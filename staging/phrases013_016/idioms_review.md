# Idiom candidate review

Raw authored pool: 662 entries. Accepted after this review: 484 canonical candidates, 1214 stored forms. All accepted canonical/form keys exclude every one of the frozen 7,827 phrase lookup keys. Frozen files were read only.

Burmese glosses were independently authored by language understanding. Sources were used only to check English lexical status, variants, and sense distinctions; no proprietary Burmese glosses were copied.

Forms are explicit reviewed surface strings. Reflexive/possessive idioms use matching finite pronouns, rather than artificial third-person strings such as “blows your top” when the possessive denotes the subject. Ordinary mass nouns have no invented plurals. Alternative established variants remain one owner.

Editorial decisions:

- No truncated parts of long idioms such as fly by the seat, take the bull by, or hit the nail on were retained.
- Closed single words such as wallflower, birdbrain, copycat, workhorse, and loudmouth were rejected as spaced candidates.
- Kept substantive fixed idioms such as smoking gun, elephant in the room, red herring, house of cards, rat race, and social butterfly.
- Retained established colloquial and some British idioms when useful in film/TV dialogue; excluded a smaller set of dated regional forms.
- Two phrasal verbs (chicken out, suck up to) are marked accurately rather than labeled idiom.

- do the honors (replaced): The original gloss reverses agency: the person performs a hosting or ceremonial task, rather than merely being invited to start something.
- eat your hat (replaced): The original gloss reverses the condition: the speaker promises an absurd act if their prediction is wrong, not if it is right. The idiom normally appears as a conditional assurance; remove the mechanically inflected ate/eats/eating surfaces and keep useful base possessor variants.
