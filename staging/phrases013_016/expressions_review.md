# Reviewed fixed expression pool

All Burmese glosses were independently authored. The raw pool was deliberately overinclusive and was pruned for established units, current subtitle usefulness, complete contiguous wording, the 2–5 token limit, frozen key ownership, and article/other variant duplication. No frozen asset was changed.

Priority: relationships, social character descriptions, emotional states, fixed conversational/adverbial expressions, and common figurative noun units. No incomplete long idiom was truncated to fit the token limit. Countable noun and pronoun variants were not counted as separate new canonical units.

Publisher checks:
- [Cambridge: baggage](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/baggage): established emotional baggage collocation.
- [Oxford Advanced American: arm candy](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/american_english/arm-candy): established informal expression.
- [Dictionary.com: just deserts spelling](https://www.dictionary.com/articles/just-deserts-vs-just-desserts): deserts is the canonical spelling; no desserts variant stored.

Editorial exclusions included malformed open compounds (wall flower, chatter box, worry wart, cheap skate, life blood, death bed), invented/non-unit phrases (labor of hate, saving face, feather in the nest), incomplete long idioms, compositional larger than variants, pronoun substitutions of the same unit, low-value dated expressions, and phrases reserved by the idiom agent. Established compound spellings are not silently converted into phrase entries.

Raw authored candidates: 1143. Final reviewed entries: 651. Frozen canonical/form or normalized article-unit rejections: 1. Idiom-agent overlaps excluded: 41.

Final pool contains no verbal idiom requiring verb inflections. Fixed expressions use empty forms. 173 useful noun plurals were individually listed and reviewed, including irregular yes men, love children, better halves, rites of passage, and pillars of the community. Initial indefinite articles were removed where the bare 2–5-token noun unit is established; this does not add canonical variants. Frozen-key and internal head/form ownership checks passed after these changes.

Final supplementary review: removed one-word lexical units that had been incorrectly spaced (know-it-all, hands-on, off-color, free-for-all, deadwood), marginal dated/regional terms, and transparent technical noun labels with limited independent phrase lookup value. Correct canonical hyphenation is retained for one-night stand, long-distance relationship, open-door policy, open-and-shut case, open-ended question, footloose and fancy-free, and heart-to-heart talk. Unhyphenated common subtitle variants are stored as forms where the unit remains 2–5 tokens.

Additional publisher checks:
- [Cambridge: death stare](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/death-stare): established informal noun, with useful plural death stares.
- [Cambridge: one-night stand](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/pronunciation/english/one-night-stand): canonical hyphenation.
- [Oxford: hot to trot](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/trot_1): eager to start an activity or sexually eager; both concise senses authored independently.

Final handoff totals after supplementary review: 616 canonicals, 172 stored forms, 788 lookup keys; type counts {'expression': 375, 'idiom': 241}. All frozen-key/internal-ownership, token-limit, schema, lowercase/whitespace, and Burmese non-ASCII checks passed.
