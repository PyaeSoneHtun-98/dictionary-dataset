# Verbs and domain-expression draft review

This is an independently authored staging pool, not a batch or release artifact. No frozen source, lookup, manifest, or artifact was modified.

## Scope and checks

- Original manually authored pool: 495 entries (289 phrasal/action verb candidates plus 206 domain expressions).
- Accepted after editorial review: 452 entries, comprising 264 phrasal verbs, 176 expressions, and 12 idioms.
- Accepted stored forms: 865; lookup strings: 1,317.
- Every canonical and stored form was checked against the frozen 7,827-key phrase namespace. No overlap was found.
- Canonicals and forms were checked for 2–5 tokens, normalized lowercase whitespace, local collisions, and 1–3 Burmese-only meanings. No errors were found.
- Other staging JSON drafts were checked at review time; no canonical overlap existed then. The coordinator must still cross-filter the combined final pool.
- Forms were supplied using explicitly written, manually reviewed conjugation tables, not generic suffix rules. Reviewed irregulars include bid/bid, cast/cast, cling/clung, creep/crept, dive/dived/dove, eat/ate/eaten, freeze/froze/frozen, grind/ground, ring/rang/rung, sell/sold, spin/spun, string/strung, sweep/swept, win/won, and wring/wrung. American leveled/leveling and squirreled/squirreling are used.
- A canonical whose normal construction requires an inserted object was rejected when a useful contiguous surface was unsupported. Separable verbs with real contiguous object-after-particle or passive uses remain eligible; no placeholders or separated-object forms were added.

## Editorial decisions

- Removed `chalk up to` and `milk for`: their intended constructions need an intervening object. Kept `chalk up` for recording/achieving success.
- Removed `tide over`: Oxford specifies an intervening person and no passive, making the bare contiguous surface unsuitable for this matcher.
- Removed `need to know` and `need to know basis`: the secrecy gloss could mistranslate ordinary literal speech, and the dictionary idiom is `on a need-to-know basis`.
- Removed `hard reset`: publisher usage supports factory restoration while actual technical usage can vary; retained the clearer `factory reset`.
- Removed `held without bail` as a separate canonical; retained it as an inflection of `hold without bail`.
- Removed `suppressing fire`, retaining the conventional `suppressive fire`.
- Removed `back door` for computer intrusion because `backdoor` is the relevant conventional closed spelling.
- Removed low-value, disputed, overly technical, or weakly fixed candidates: brave out, bash out, batten down, box out, bundle off, cast aside, chance upon, creep up, crumble away, edge in, fling off, fling out, fly off, hive off, peg out, plump for, steam up, swoop down, full alert, zero day, operating capital, bottom dollar, call of duty, burn notice, danger close, castle doctrine, stand your ground law, pawn off, bat away, brute force, mission critical, mutually assured destruction, fruit of the poisonous tree, string out.
- Classified `screech to a halt` as expression. Figurative noun phrases such as smoking gun, hired gun, nuclear option, golden parachute, golden handshake, poison pill, white knight, red tape, rubber stamp, blank check, deep pockets, and blood money are idioms. Established legal/military/business noun expressions use expression.
- Kept American and British conversational vocabulary when realistically useful in films, including pony up, suss out, fob off, and cock up.
- `beam up` and `beam down` carry science-fiction transport senses; their Burmese glosses explicitly mention transport to/from a spaceship.
- Transformed bare `line of duty` into the conventional contiguous expression `in the line of duty`.
- Legal terms describe ordinary subtitle senses without claiming that every jurisdiction uses the same legal procedure. Glosses were independently authored in Burmese; no proprietary dictionary definitions or translations were copied.

## Publisher dictionary checks

These pages were used to check lexical status, construction, or selected senses only:

- [Oxford: beam up](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/beam-up) — science-fiction transport to/from a spaceship; supports down/up particles.
- [Oxford: pony up](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/pony-up) — American informal payment sense.
- [Oxford: square away](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/square-away) — organization/completion sense and usually passive construction.
- [Oxford: rope in](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/rope-in) — persuasion to participate; usual passive yields a contiguous useful stored form.
- [Oxford: chalk up](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/chalk-up) — achieve/record sense and contiguous object-after-particle construction.
- [Oxford: tide over](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/tide-over) — intervening object and no passive; rejected.
- [Cambridge: screech to a halt](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/screech-to-a-halt) — established collocation, not phrasal verb.
- [Cambridge: milk](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/milk?topic=dairy-products-and-eggs) — exploitation sense exists, but does not establish the proposed contiguous `milk for`; rejected.
- [Oxford: need-to-know](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/need-to-know) and [Cambridge: on a need-to-know basis](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/on-a-need-to-know-basis) — proper secrecy construction; bare `need to know` rejected.
- [Cambridge: reset](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/reset?topic=changing) — hard-reset example entails erasing applications; ambiguous draft rejected.

The attempted publisher lookups for burn notice, danger close, and pawn off did not establish suitable entries; none was retained.
